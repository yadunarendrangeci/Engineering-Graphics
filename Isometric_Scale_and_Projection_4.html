Isometric scale and isometric projection

Seven slides. Use the buttons, the numbered tabs or the ← → keys. 2D drawings are on the left and 3D models on the right; drag 3D models to rotate them.

1 · Why isometric lengths are 82% of true lengths

In an isometric projection the cube is viewed along its body diagonal, so every edge is foreshortened equally. The top face, a true square DPB (sides at 45° to the diagonal DB), appears as the rhombus DAB (sides at 30°). Since DB is common, DA / DP = cos 45° / cos 30° = 0.816: isometric length ≈ 82% of true length.

Thin purple = true length (45°); thick = isometric length (30°). DA/DP is computed live.

2 · A cube: multiview and isometric projection

The multiview projection needs two views (front and top). The isometric projection shows three faces (left, top, right) in one view. Its three axes x, y, z are 120° apart; x and y are drawn at 30° to the horizontal and z vertical, so the front vertical edge meets the base edges at 90° + 30°.

Turn the model and press "Isometric view": the picture snaps to the isometric projection on the left.

3 · A rectangular prism

Length is measured along the x axis (30° to the right), width along the y axis (30° to the left) and height along z (vertical). Lines parallel to these axes are isometric lines and are the only lines along which measurements are made.

4 · Construction of the isometric scale

  1. From D draw a line at 45° to the horizontal and mark true lengths 0, 1, 2, 3 … on it (the true-length scale).
  2. From D draw a line at 30° to the horizontal (the isometric scale).
  3. From each true-length division drop a vertical line to meet the 30° line. The divisions obtained are isometric lengths: each is cos 45°/cos 30° = 0.816 of the true division.

Isometric scale: every true length on the 45° line drops to 0.816 of itself on the 30° line.

The same 45° (true) and 30° (isometric) lines appear on the top face of a cube: DP is true, DA is isometric.

5 · Simplified isometric scale

  1. Draw a horizontal line AB and mark the true lengths 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 on it.
  2. From A draw a line at 15° to AB.
  3. From each division draw a line at 45° to AB (leaning back towards A) to meet the 15° line. Since the angles of the triangle are 15°, 45° and 120°, each length on the 15° line is sin 45°/sin 120° = 0.816 of the true length.

Each isometric length on the 15° line = 0.816 × the true length (sine rule: sin 45° / sin 120°).

6 · Isometric projection by the box method

  1. Enclose the object (multiview a) in a transparent box of its overall length, width and height, and draw the box in isometric (b).
  2. Locate points on the box edges with measurements taken from the views: Q at height z on the left edge, P at distance x from the right end of the top edge.
  3. Join the points, darken the outline of the object and leave the box as thin construction lines (c).

7 · Isometric projection by the coordinate method

  1. Take the origin at the front-left corner a of the top view and draw the base as an isometric rectangle ABC (b): AB along x at 30°, AC along y at 30°.
  2. Locate each point by its coordinates: measure x along AB to fix the foot of P, then measure the height z vertically (a coordinate line) to get P; Q is found the same way from its height above A.
  3. Draw the parallel edges through the points along the y axis, join them and darken the outline (c).

8 · Isometric projection versus isometric view (drawing) of a cube

Both pictures have the same shape: a regular hexagon with the axes at 30°. The isometric projection is the true orthographic projection, so every edge along the axes is drawn with the isometric scale, 0.816 × true length. The isometric view (isometric drawing) ignores the foreshortening and uses true lengths, so it is 1/0.816 = 1.225 times (22.5%) larger than the projection. Being quicker to draw, the isometric view is used in practice; the projection is the "correct" size.
